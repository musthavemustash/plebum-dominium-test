If you get stuck

Time10 min
DifficultyEasy

You will need: Nothing, find your question

Find the question that matches your problem. When in doubt, stop is always a valid step.

What am I actually making?

Three keys, made at home with dice, on a computer with no internet. Any two can spend; one cannot. The keys are stamped into titanium and kept in three places. A hardware wallet is optional, and it never creates the keys.

I already own a hardware wallet. Do I start there?

No. It stays in the drawer until the plates exist and a small payment has moved. If a key later goes on a device, the second key goes on a different maker’s device. All three never go on hardware. The plates are the backup.

The download is newer than the version on the sheet.

Stop. Sparrow and Electrum must match the version on the sheet exactly. A newer Sparrow or Electrum file is a stop. Delete it. Tails is different. Download the latest Tails 7.x release from tails.net, and verify its signature and checksum. If either check fails, or if any boot step differs from the guide, stop. Delete the file. A Tails 8 release is not covered. It needs a guide update first.

The two word lists don’t match.

Stop. Don’t pick the nicer list. Don’t “fix” word 24. Roll again or find the copying mistake. A fixed word is a destroyed key.

Can I keep the words in a password manager?

No. A photo, note, password manager, or email is one hacked account from the whole seed. Words go on metal. The map is encrypted; its passphrase is on paper, away from the plates.

What if the map, or its passphrase, is lost?

Three plates rebuild the map exactly, so the coins aren’t gone. But two plates cannot. The third key can’t be derived from the other two. Back up the passphrase paper in two places, away from the plates. (Full rule on the map sheet.)

What does my family do?

Hand them the “If I am gone” sheet. Two plates and the map. A Jade and a BitBox from the makers’ own shops, one plate per device. No Tails. No typing words into a phone.
