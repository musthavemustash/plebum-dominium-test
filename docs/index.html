Absolute Ownership by the Common People

“Pleb” is an everyday Bitcoiner. “Dominium” is Latin for absolute ownership. This site is for the Bitcoiner who wants to build a secure, self-custody Bitcoin wallet from the ground up, bypassing the need for a company, an app, or a custodian to manage or store access to their funds.

This guide shows you how to generate entropy with dice, import it into verified software, and stamp a 2-of-3 multisig wallet into titanium. Hardware wallets are completely optional.

What this is

A guide to holding your own Bitcoin

Plebum Dominium is a step-by-step guide to Bitcoin self-custody at home. You generate keys with dice, verify every file you download, and stamp the words into titanium. No company holds your coins. No account can freeze them.

Three paths cover three levels of effort. Pick the one that fits your situation on the next page.

Why this guide

Single point of failure

A single point of failure is any instance that can potentially compromise the wallet.

What this guide protects against

  • Exchange fails: The coins are in a wallet you control.
  • Weak device randomness: The seed is made with dice you can watch.
  • Device stops working: The plate holds the same words.
  • Word copied wrong: Two tools must agree before stamping.

What it does not protect against

  • Plate stolen alone: The plate alone can restore the full wallet. There is no passphrase to stop it. This is the major point of failure on this path.
  • Device fails or is tampered with: One device and its maker are the whole trust.
  • Seed stolen alone: Anyone with the 24 words holds the coins.
  • Plate and device together: Together they are the wallet.
  • Words kept anywhere except titanium and the chosen device: Words are only stamped into titanium and loaded onto the chosen device per the guide.

Why this guide

Single point of failure

A single point of failure is any instance that can potentially compromise the wallet.

What this guide protects against

  • One plate stolen: One key alone opens nothing.
  • One house lost: Two plates plus the map rebuild the wallet.
  • Exchange fails: The coins are in a wallet you control.
  • Weak device randomness: The keys are made with dice, offline.
  • Device flaw or tamper: Devices never create the keys.
  • Word copied wrong: Two tools must agree before stamping.

What it does not protect against

  • Two plates in one place: The separation is gone.
  • Map passphrase lost, with only two plates: Two plates cannot rebuild the map.
  • Two plates lost: One key alone cannot spend.
  • Passphrase on the 2-of-3: That passphrase is a single point in disguise.
  • Words kept anywhere except titanium and the chosen device: Words are only stamped into titanium and loaded onto the chosen device per the guide.
  • Heirs cannot find the plates and map: The inheritance sheet only helps if they can find it.
