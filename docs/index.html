About the name

Plebum Dominium

PLAY-boom doh-MIN-ee-oom

A Latin-inspired name. “Pleb” refers to an everyday Bitcoiner and draws on Latin plebs, the common people. Dominium means ownership or dominion.

The name expresses “Ownership by the common people.” Here, that means holding your own Bitcoin and controlling the keys.

Hold your own Bitcoin with a plan you can understand, verify, and recover.

Plebum Dominium is a practical guide to self-custody: choosing a setup, checking software, protecting backups, and rehearsing a spend.

The work ahead

Build confidence one step at a time

Understand

Identify the failures you need to survive. Choose a setup you and your recovery helper can maintain.

Verify

Check software sources, wallet details, and backup records. A second tool is useful only when the check is meaningfully independent.

Protect

Keep signing secrets offline and plan separate backup locations. Record the public wallet configuration needed for recovery.

Rehearse

Restore, receive, and spend with test funds. Write instructions that still make sense when the original device is gone.

Two approaches

Choose the tradeoffs you can manage

More components can distribute risk and also create more ways to make a mistake. Neither setup is automatically the safest for everyone.

One key + a passphrase

A simpler signing arrangement with an additional recovery secret. Both the seed backup and exact passphrase must remain available.

Watch for: a forgotten passphrase, compromised signer, or backups that are stored together.

Three keys · 2-of-3

Two signing keys authorize a spend. Proper separation can tolerate one unavailable key, but recovery also depends on the complete wallet configuration.

Watch for: shared-device compromise, missing public-key records, and recovery complexity.

Dice can supply seed entropy when used correctly. They do not make a compromised computer or signing implementation safe.
