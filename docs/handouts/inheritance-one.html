Help a trusted person understand what to find—and when to stop.

Leave a clear private plan

Describe the wallet type, the location of the full instructions, the required recovery items, and a trusted contact who can explain the process. Keep signing secrets out of this website, email, and the general instruction sheet.

Separate ownership and access arrangements from the technical recovery instructions. Make sure the intended person can find the plan when needed.

Identify the required items

This path requires the seed backup and the exact wallet passphrase.

Record the wallet software, network, script type, account and derivation path. Preserve a verified public descriptor if your wallet supports exporting one.

The passphrase cannot be reset by a wallet company. A wallet opened without it, or with a typo, may show a different balance. Do not assume that balance represents all of the owner’s funds.

Confirm the recovered wallet

Follow the recorded, rehearsed procedure and verify the policy and wallet details against independent trusted records. An address match alone does not establish that a coordinator is trustworthy.

If the wallet is empty or differs from the records, stop. Check the passphrase, account, network, derivation information and synchronization before concluding funds are lost. Do not overwrite backups or create replacement keys as a guess.

Practice before an emergency

Walk through locating the records and explaining the steps with the intended helper. Use a disposable test wallet for hands-on training, without exposing the owner’s signing secrets.

When a legitimate recovery is needed, independently verify the destination and transaction details on trusted signing equipment. A small successful transfer is useful evidence, but it is not a complete audit of the recovery setup.

Protect the secrets: no legitimate support agent needs a seed phrase or private key. If the instructions, equipment compatibility, or wallet identity are uncertain, stop before entering secrets or signing.
