Plan durable seed backups and preserve the complete wallet configuration.

Before the spending rehearsal: your practice wallet remains TEST ONLY. Use fictional records or disposable test copies while checking the backup process. A future intended wallet needs fresh keys, its own verified configuration, and checks of its actual backups before funding; a successful test-wallet recovery does not verify those records. The tools remain under public review.

Record accurately

Back up each of the three seeds separately on metal using the plate maker’s recording instructions. Preserve word order and any required encoding information. Work privately and compare every entry against the original.

Keep the original protected while checking the replacement. A damaged or ambiguous mark needs correction before you rely on it.

Check recovery safely

Read the metal record back, then restore it with verified offline software in a fresh Tails session. For multisig, load only one seed per boot and use the complete public configuration. Compare the recorded wallet settings and several full addresses, then rehearse signing on the designated test network. Do not wipe your only working signer to test an unproven backup. Never type seeds into this website or an online form.

For a rehearsal, use a disposable test wallet. For an existing funded wallet, plan the verification carefully before changing devices or backups.

Keep the complete recovery information

Preserve a verified public descriptor or compatible wallet-configuration backup containing all three cosigners’ public information, key origins, derivation paths and the 2-of-3 policy. Two seeds alone do not supply the missing cosigner’s public information.

A public-only descriptor cannot sign by itself, but it reveals wallet information. Confirm that the export contains no private keys before treating it as public data. Protect its availability and privacy.

Plan copies and locations

Balance loss, theft, access, and disaster risks. More copies can improve availability and also expose secrets in more places. Document which locations contain which material; do not combine enough signing secrets in one place to defeat your separation plan.

Do not destroy an original merely because a new backup looks correct. Retire it only after a successful recovery check and a deliberate review of the remaining copies. Revisit the plan when locations or helpers change.

Stop if: a backup is unreadable, the recovered wallet differs, or any required passphrase or configuration is missing. Do not overwrite or discard the remaining working material.
